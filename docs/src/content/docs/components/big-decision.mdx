`BigDecision` presents one consequential choice as a comparison a reviewer can actually check: a criteria matrix when the options share comparable dimensions, or a substantive option list when prose rationale carries the argument.
Place it in the plan section where the decision belongs; the smaller end-of-plan questions belong in [SmallDecisionSet](/components/small-decision-set/) instead.
Open decisions mark the author's recommendation, while decided decisions preserve the chosen outcome and every alternative.
A decision can rate its reversibility on a fixed scale with a built-in explainer, and wide matrices scroll inside their card without widening the page.

## When to use it

Use `BigDecision` when a choice carries real tradeoffs a reviewer must weigh before implementation.
Phrase the decision as a question, then name the criteria it actually turns on - the forces from the context paragraph - explain each one in its body, and score every option against every criterion.
Keep verdicts terse: a verdict word plus a short qualifier, with longer reasoning behind the cell's info tooltip.
Drop a criterion whose verdicts match across every option; a row that discriminates nothing is noise.
Treat "do nothing" as an ordinary option whenever the status quo is a legitimate choice, and state the decision's reversibility - what it costs to change course later is the fact a reviewer most needs.
When the decision lands, mark the chosen option and keep the losing options visible so the outcome retains its alternatives.

### Matrix or plain options?

`BigDecision` renders two shapes, chosen by whether the decision declares criteria:

- **Declare criteria and score them** when the options compete on shared, comparable dimensions - architecture and technology choices usually do. The matrix makes the comparison checkable.
- **Omit criteria for a plain option list** when the choice is qualitative or the options do not share dimensions - naming, tone, or direction calls where prose rationale carries the argument. The options render as substantive cards with summaries and detail drawers.
- If the choice is quick enough that a sentence per option settles it, it belongs in a [SmallDecisionSet](/components/small-decision-set/) instead.

Forcing a matrix onto options with no shared criteria produces rows that discriminate nothing; forcing prose onto a real tradeoff hides the comparison. Pick the shape that matches the decision.

### When not to use it

- Small end-of-plan questions with short answers - use [SmallDecisionSet](/components/small-decision-set/)
- Implementation steps with no competing choice - use prose or a task list
- A decision needing sign-off from a named approver - agreement flows arrive with the live review layer

## Use cases

- Ask for agreement on an open architecture choice while making the criteria, the recommendation, and the accepted downsides checkable
- Record a decided choice without erasing the alternatives that reviewers considered
- Preserve a deferred choice and its current context so later work can resume from a clear boundary

## Usage

````mdx
<BigDecision question="Which persistence layer should back review comments?" status="open">

Comments need durable identities and ordered replies without blocking a later multi-reviewer workflow.

<Criterion title="Anchor integrity">

Selection anchors and their threads must stay consistent through crashes.

</Criterion>

<Criterion title="Local-first setup" />

<Option title="PostgreSQL" recommended summary="The relational store the team already operates.">

<Score criterion="Anchor integrity" verdict="Strong" tone="good">

Transactions keep a thread and its anchor in one atomic write.

</Score>

<Score criterion="Local-first setup" verdict="Needs a server" tone="bad" />

</Option>

<Option title="SQLite" summary="One embedded database beside the local server.">

<Score criterion="Anchor integrity" verdict="Strong" tone="good" />

<Score criterion="Local-first setup" verdict="Zero setup" tone="good" />

</Option>

<Reversibility rating="somewhat-hard">

The repository layer isolates SQL, so swapping engines later costs a data migration.

</Reversibility>

</BigDecision>
````

## Authoring

### BigDecision

| Attribute       | Type                              | Required | Behavior                                                            |
| --------------- | --------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `question` | string (non-empty)                | Yes      | Renders the question in the card header.    |
| `status`   | enum: `open` `decided` `deferred` | No       | Sets the lifecycle state; `decided` and `deferred` render a pill. Defaults to `open`. |

Markdown before the first scoped child supplies the context and forces that make the choice meaningful.
Each decision needs at least two options.

### Criterion

Declare one `Criterion` per row of the matrix, in the order the rows should read.

| Attribute | Type               | Required | Behavior                                                       |
| --------- | ------------------ | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `title`   | string (non-empty) | Yes      | Names the row; titles must be unique within the decision.      |

Markdown in the body becomes the criterion's native detail disclosure - why it matters - behind the row title.
The body is optional but strongly recommended: an unexplained criterion asks the reviewer to trust the row instead of checking it.
Reserve bare criteria for terms the plan's audience could not possibly read two ways.
A decision with no criteria renders its options as a stacked list without a matrix.

### Option

| Attribute     | Type               | Required | Behavior                                                     |
| ------------- | ------------------ | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `title`       | string (non-empty) | Yes      | Names the column; titles must be unique within the decision. |
| `summary`     | string             | No       | The one-sentence rationale under the option name.            |
| `recommended` | bare boolean       | No       | Marks at most one option with a Recommended pill.             |
| `chosen`      | bare boolean       | No       | Marks the single outcome of a `decided` decision.            |

Markdown in an option body outside its scores becomes a Details drawer inside the option card.

### Details

At most one `Details` element holds question-level long-form content; it renders as a Details drawer under the context prose.
An option gets its own Details drawer from ordinary markdown in the `Option` body outside its `Score` children; do not nest a `Details` element inside an option.

### Score

Every option scores every declared criterion exactly once.

| Attribute   | Type                                  | Required | Behavior                                                 |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| `criterion` | string matching a declared criterion  | Yes      | Binds the cell to its row.                               |
| `verdict`   | string, at most 32 characters         | Yes      | The cell text: a verdict word plus a short qualifier.    |
| `tone`      | enum: `good` `bad` `mixed` `neutral`  | No       | Colors the cell's icon; defaults to `neutral`.           |

Markdown in the body becomes the cell's tooltip for reasoning the verdict cannot carry.
A decided decision requires exactly one chosen option, and chosen options are invalid in open or deferred decisions.

### Reversibility

At most one `Reversibility` element rates what changing course later would cost; it renders as an always-visible section below the options, with the built-in explainer tooltip on its label.

| Attribute | Type                                        | Required | Behavior                                     |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| `rating`  | enum: `easy` `somewhat-hard` `hard`         | Yes      | Renders the fixed rating phrase and its tint. |

Markdown in the body is the decision-specific rationale shown under the rating.

`Criterion`, `Details`, `Option`, and `Reversibility` must be direct `BigDecision` children, and `Score` must be a direct `Option` child.
Their markdown bodies accept prose, lists, inline formatting, and plain fenced code, but reject headings, footnotes, and typed components with positional diagnostics.

## Lifecycle

A decision moves through three static statuses:

- **open** (the default) shows no status pill and may mark one recommendation.
- **decided** shows the pill, requires exactly one chosen option, and leads with the chosen outcome while retaining the alternatives.
- **deferred** shows the pill and preserves the authored context for revisiting the choice.

Option selection, criterion weighting, lifecycle actions, and full-screen viewing belong to the forthcoming live review application.
