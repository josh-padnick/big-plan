Recommendation and implemented proposal

Keep long contents usable without hiding the plan

Bound the desktop contents container to the viewport, keep its header fixed, let the section list scroll independently, and reveal the active section only when the reader's position changes.

Recommendation: Ship the combined bounded-scroll plus active-reveal behavior. It fixes arbitrary access and position tracking with one stable navigation model, without making plan authors group sections or readers manage another collapse state.

Approaches considered

One foundation, one useful complement

The credible approaches solve different parts of the problem. A viewport bound restores access to every link, while active reveal preserves orientation during reading.

Strong foundation

Viewport-bounded sticky container

Keep the TOC in its familiar desktop position and make only the section list an independent, overscroll-contained region.

Tradeoff: Nested scrolling requires a visible, predictable boundary and must not move the document when the pointer is over the list.

Not recommended

Collapse by section or part

Reduce the visible list by folding groups and asking the reader to open the relevant one.

Tradeoff: It hides destinations, adds state to manage, and depends on plan structure that many long plans do not have.

Useful complement

Auto-reveal the active section

Move the TOC list just enough to keep the scroll-spy's current section inside its visible region.

Tradeoff: Alone, this tracks reading but still leaves arbitrary offscreen destinations unreachable. It should run only when the active section changes so it does not fight manual TOC browsing.

Recommended combination

Bounded list plus active reveal

Combine direct access to all destinations with automatic orientation as the reader advances through the plan.

Why this wins: It handles long ungrouped and grouped plans consistently, preserves the familiar Contents header, and keeps manual and automatic movement from competing.
