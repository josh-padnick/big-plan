Big Plan turns your agent's standard text plan into a rich, inert HTML document designed for
human review. It runs on your machine, needs no account, and the plan file on your disk stays
the source of truth.

Run it in your favourite agent harness as a skill:

## Plans are a big deal

The big decisions happen in the plan, while they are still cheap to change. Once an agent starts
executing, changing course means unwinding work; while the plan is on the table, changing
everything costs one comment.

The more confident you are in the plan, the better your agent performs at execution and the
faster your final review goes. That confidence comes from review, and review quality is set by
how the plan presents itself.

## The wall of text problem

By default an agent's plan arrives as thousands of words of Markdown, scrolled past in a
terminal or skimmed in a raw diff. **The most consequential document in the workflow is
routinely the least readable one.** When reading the plan is painful, review quietly collapses
into rubber-stamping, and the decisions that matter most go unexamined.

We think of those plans of limited ambition as small plans. If plans matter this much to the
human, they should present in a first-class way — and those are big plans.

## What makes a good agent plan

1. **Easy to scan.** You don't always want to read the whole plan. Sometimes it is better to
   scan the sections and read the details where they interest you.
2. **Easy to read.** Sometimes a paragraph is best, but often a visual arrangement conveys the
   same information with far less cognitive load.
3. **Easy to update.** It is painful to reference a specific paragraph of prose. You should be
   able to highlight a section and give the agent feedback on it.
4. **Token-efficient.** Plans get written, revised, and re-read by agents, and every one of
   those interactions costs tokens.
5. **Versioned.** Sometimes the plan updates and you are not sure what changed.
6. **Local and private.** Your plan can contain sensitive information; it should not be shared
   with anyone else without a good reason.

## Philosophy

Big Plan makes two bets that shape everything else.

**A plan should be structured, not freeform.** Plans are MDX with a fixed library of typed
components, so every plan renders into the same document shape. The agent spends its effort on
the decisions rather than the presentation, and the tenth plan you review reads exactly like the
first.

**A plan on your own disk should not require an account.** One standalone command renders it,
the output is one HTML file, and nothing about reviewing it involves logging in or leaving your
machine.

## What Big Plan is not

- **Not code review.** Code review judges a change after the work is done, when pushing back is
  expensive. Big Plan reviews intent before any code exists.
- **Not project management.** No tickets, statuses, or boards. It exists only for the moment of
  agreement.
- **Not a Markdown preview.** A preview shows you the text. Big Plan is built for review:
  section navigation, readable code, typed components, anchored comments, and approval.
- **Not a service.** Everything runs on your machine and the rendered file makes no external
  requests.
