# Review chrome: marks, controls, and layered surfaces

Big Plan's own review surface is full of chrome a wireframe used to have to fake, and this document draws all of it with the vocabulary that exists for it.

Copy marks on a snippet, a settings gear in the toolbar, a launcher that opens a menu, and a confirmation that covers the page it is about to change: each one is authored rather than approximated, so a reviewer argues about the product instead of about the drawing.

## The reviewer's toolbar

The toolbar carries the plan's identity on the left and the controls on the right, which is the split every real application uses and the one a row of loose children cannot produce on its own.

A `Row` distributes space between its children, so a title and three buttons each take a quarter of the bar.
Wrapping each side in a `Group` makes the row two items, and `justify="between"` sends them to the two ends.

The marks are named rather than typed.
A gear is `icon="settings"`, and `iconOnly` keeps the words as the control's accessible name and tooltip instead of drawing them.

## What each screen settles

The launcher and the confirmation are the same element carrying two different settings, and each setting answers its own question.

`backdrop` answers whether the page is still available: the menu leaves it `clear` because nothing about the page became unusable, and the confirmation dims it because the reader has to answer before anything else can happen.
`kind` answers what the surface is: the menu is an ordinary `dialog`, and the confirmation is an `alert`, which is what guards a removal that does not come back.

Drawing either as an ordinary panel in the column would have hidden both answers behind an arrangement neither surface actually has.
