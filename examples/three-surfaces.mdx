# Retire the nightly export job

Replace the nightly bulk export with a change feed, so downstream teams read
fresh records instead of yesterday's snapshot.

<QuickSummary>

<Why>

- Every downstream report carries a stale-data caveat, because the nightly
  export is up to a day behind and now finishes late.

</Why>

<What>

- Publish a change feed from the orders service, and give each consumer a
  cursor it owns.

</What>

<How>

- Prove the contract with one consumer, then move the rest and retire the job.

</How>

</QuickSummary>

<Part title="Context" />

## Where the day goes

*The nightly window is full, and the data it produces is already old.*

The export starts at 01:00 and now finishes between 04:40 and 05:20. Three
pipelines wait on it, so a late finish moves their start into the working day.

## What good looks like

*Downstream reads are minutes behind, not a day behind.*

A consumer sees an order change within five minutes of it happening, and can
replay from any point in the last seven days without asking for a backfill.

<Part title="The proposal" />

## The change feed

*The orders service publishes every change once, in order.*

The feed carries one record per change, keyed by order id and stamped with a
monotonic sequence number. Nothing else in the service changes.

## Cursors and replay

*Each consumer owns its position, so one slow reader never blocks another.*

A cursor is a sequence number and a timestamp. A consumer commits its cursor
after it has durably handled a record, and can rewind to any retained point.

<Part title="Delivery" />

## Moving the consumers

*One consumer proves the contract before the other three follow.*

Reporting moves first because it tolerates replay most easily. Billing moves
last because its reconciliation needs the nightly export as a cross-check until
the feed has run clean for a full week.

## Acceptance criteria

*The nightly job is retired only when all four hold.*

- Every consumer reads from the feed and commits a cursor.
- A replay from seven days back reproduces the same downstream totals.
- The feed's end-to-end delay stays under five minutes for a full week.
- The nightly window is empty for seven consecutive nights.
