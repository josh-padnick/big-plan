# Decision family reconciliation

The approved interaction design should survive, but the public components should be named for the reader jobs they perform rather than the arrangements they render.

<QuickSummary>

<Why>

- Asking, auditing, and batching are stable reader jobs; rows, matrix, and brief are presentation choices.

</Why>

<What>

- Recommend `Decision`, `DecisionAnalysis`, and `QuickDecisionSet`, each carrying forward one approved visual treatment.

</What>

<How>

- Preserve the ask-versus-record seam and keep presentation primitives internal.

</How>

</QuickSummary>

<Part title="Recommendation" />

## Recommendation

_Name public components for why the reader opened them; treat rows, matrix, and brief as presentation decisions inside those jobs._

| Public component | Reader job | Approved form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Decision` | Ask for and confirm one answer | 2.1 option rows |
| `DecisionAnalysis` | Audit analysis or an outcome | 3.1 keyed matrix |
| `QuickDecisionSet` | Batch genuinely low-cost picks | 4.1 compact hierarchy |

This is not a four-component family. `DecisionMatrix` and `DecisionBrief` describe clothes, not jobs. The same matrix can either ask for a choice or document an analysis, while a brief can still be too consequential to batch. Those names would move the most important distinction out of the API and into prose.

The recommendation preserves the round-2 ruling's durable seam: `Decision` asks; the analysis component records. It changes one part of the approved exploration deliberately: the weighted keyed matrix becomes an analysis surface rather than another direct-selection mode of `Decision`.

<Part title="Strawman verdict" />

## Strawman verdict

_The captain's strawman identifies the right visual ingredients, but two names and one mode flag obscure the component model._

- **`DecisionAnalysis` for tough decisions — adopt the name, defined by job.** Analysis is stable; relative toughness is not.
- **One `DecisionBrief` per slide — reject as a batching replacement.** It turns the observed 25-question corpus into repeated navigation and answer chrome.
- **`DecisionMatrix` — keep the keyed matrix inside `DecisionAnalysis`.** The public name must say whether the reader is choosing or auditing.
- **`DecisionBrief` — reuse the hierarchy, not the public name.** Briefness does not reveal whether a component asks, records, or batches.
- **`useComplexMode` — reject in favor of a separate component.** The flag changes grammar, lifecycle, and interaction—not just styling.

### Why `QuickDecisionSet` is not redundant

The taxonomy census found six real sets containing 25 questions. Thirteen are truly label-only; twelve are heavier and should be promoted to `Decision` or `DecisionAnalysis` rather than hidden. The remaining low-cost questions still benefit from one numbered scan, one compact context, and one batch completion boundary.

One brief per slide would preserve each question but destroy the set-level job. It would also multiply navigation and review overhead around decisions whose defining characteristic is that they do not deserve that much ceremony.

### Why a boolean mode is the wrong seam

`useComplexMode` would not toggle one presentation detail. It would introduce criteria, complete score coverage, explanations, reversibility, lifecycle state, and different answer behavior. That is a different authoring grammar and reader contract. If a single universal root is ever preferred, it should use an explicit discriminated model such as `kind="choice" | "analysis"`—and that would openly reverse the round-2 two-component ruling.

<Part title="Decision" />

## Decision

_Ask the reviewer to choose one answer, explain each option in place, and end with one confirmation._

### Public contract

- **`Decision`** — `question: string` is required.
- **`Option`** — `title: string` is required and unique; `recommended?: boolean` marks at most one; `summary?: string` adds one-sentence framing.
- **`Consideration`** — optional and option-local; `label: string` and `verdict: string` are required, with an optional one-sentence body.

`Decision` has no `layout`, `status`, `chosen`, `Criterion`, `Score`, or `useComplexMode` prop. The taxonomy report proposed deleting structured considerations entirely; this plan instead keeps a deliberately weaker, option-local form because the captain-approved row treatment depends on concise labels and values. There is no global criterion declaration, completeness rule, tone, or cross-option alignment. Once comparable scoring is the value, the content belongs in `DecisionAnalysis`.

## DecisionAnalysis

_Let a reviewer audit how evidence led to a recommendation or recorded outcome; do not make the analysis masquerade as a lightweight picker._

### Public contract

- **`DecisionAnalysis`** — `question: string` and `state: "proposed" | "decided" | "deferred"` are required.
- **`Criterion`** — `title: string` is required and unique; the body is a required one-sentence meaning.
- **`Option`** — `title: string` is required and unique; `recommended?: boolean`, `chosen?: boolean`, and `summary?: string` carry recommendation, outcome, and synthesis.
- **`Score`** — `criterion: string`, `verdict: string`, and `tone: "good" | "mixed" | "bad" | "neutral"` are required; the body is a one-sentence reason.
- **`Reversibility`** — `rating: "easy" | "somewhat-hard" | "hard"` and a concise body describe the cost of changing course.
- **`Details`** — optional body for deeper analysis.

Every option supplies one score for every criterion. There is no `layout` or `useComplexMode` prop, and there is no criteria-free fallback. If the author cannot justify a complete weighted comparison, the content belongs in `Decision`.

<Part title="QuickDecisionSet" />

## QuickDecisionSet

_Keep several low-cost picks in one scan and one completion boundary; promote any question that needs real analysis._

### Public contract

- **`QuickDecisionSet`** — `title?: string` and an optional one-paragraph body frame the batch.
- **`QuickDecision`** — `question: string` is required; `context?: string` is one optional sentence.
- **`Option`** — `title: string` is required and unique within the question; `recommended?: boolean` marks at most one; `summary?: string` is one optional sentence.

The set has no criteria, scores, lifecycle state, reversibility, rich option bodies, per-question proposal escape hatch, or per-question confirmation footer. The live review layer should collect one answer per question and confirm the batch once.

<Part title="Boundaries" />

## Boundaries and migration

_The recommendation preserves the important ruling, acknowledges the interaction it gives up, and defines a clean pre-release break._

### Reconciliation with round 2

- **Preserved:** `Decision` asks for an answer; `DecisionAnalysis` records or proposes an analysis. They remain separate public components.
- **Preserved:** shared implementation primitives are allowed, but they do not define the product taxonomy.
- **Changed deliberately:** the approved keyed matrix no longer doubles as a direct weighted chooser. It becomes the presentation of analysis evidence.
- **Preserved visually:** option identity stays in the full-title keyed rail; criteria and values retain one-sentence dashed-underline definitions.

The cost is real: a reviewer cannot directly choose inside a weighted matrix. The benefit is a clean boundary between “tell me what you want” and “audit how this conclusion was reached,” with no union grammar hiding behind a layout or complexity flag.

If direct selection inside a weighted matrix is non-negotiable, the honest alternative is one universal `Decision` with a discriminated `kind="choice" | "analysis"` contract. That alternative reopens the round-2 ruling and accepts a broader union model; it is still clearer than `useComplexMode`. **This plan does not recommend it** because the repository evidence supports three distinct reader jobs and the captain already approved the ask-versus-record seam.

### Internal primitives

- A selection primitive can support the answer controls used by `Decision` and the future live `QuickDecisionSet`.
- A keyed identity rail and comparison matrix can support `DecisionAnalysis` and any later evidence component that truly needs the same geometry.
- Definition popovers remain shared presentation infrastructure for criterion meanings and score reasons.

These are internal seams. They should be extracted only where there are real consumers, and none should force public components to share a grammar.

### Pre-release migration

1. Make current `Decision` rows its only public presentation and remove `layout`, lifecycle, and matrix-only authoring from its contract.
2. Rename and sharpen `ComplexDecision` to `DecisionAnalysis`; require criteria, complete score coverage, state, and reversibility.
3. Rename `SimpleDecisionSet` to `QuickDecisionSet`; keep only questions cheap enough to batch and promote the heavier twelve observed questions.
4. Retire public `brief` and `matrix` layouts. Carry their approved visual language into `QuickDecisionSet` and `DecisionAnalysis` respectively.
5. Update every example, test, machine model, and authoring guide as one clean pre-release contract change; add no compatibility aliases.

### Acceptance boundary

This artifact recommends the component model and demonstrates each target with existing renderers only. It does not implement the renamed components or their contracts. Approval here authorizes a separate implementation plan, not code changes hidden inside this reconciliation.
