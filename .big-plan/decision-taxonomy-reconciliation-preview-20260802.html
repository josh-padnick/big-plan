Decision family reconciliation

The approved interaction design should survive, but the public components should be named for the reader jobs they perform rather than the arrangements they render.

Quick summary

Why
Decision is settled; the remaining design choices are whether analysis can settle a call and whether its scoring stays qualitative or exposes weighted arithmetic.
What
Keep Decision, compare four live DecisionAnalysis variations, and show three per-call QuickDecision briefs in sequence.
How
Hold the analysis options constant while varying interaction, scale, and scoring, then repeat the approved brief without its comparison expander.

Part 1Recommendation

1.1 / Recommendation

Recommendation

Two public contracts are settled; analysis interaction and scoring remain deliberately inspectable.

Public component Reader job Approved form
Decision Ask for and confirm one answer 2.1 option rows
DecisionAnalysis Review a scored recommendation 3.1 keyed matrix, interaction and scoring to finalize
QuickDecision Resolve one low-cost call 4.1 brief minus comparison

DecisionMatrix and DecisionBrief remain internal presentation descriptions, not public roots. QuickDecision is an intentional exception to the taxonomy report's warning about relative-weight names: the captain prefers one self-contained brief call over a batch, and the narrow contract makes “quick” enforceable rather than subjective prose.

The recommendation is to keep DecisionAnalysis audit-only with qualitative scores by default because that preserves the round-2 ask-versus-record seam and avoids false numerical precision. The enabled and weighted variations are still rendered as real alternatives because each adds a coherent, independently inspectable capability.

Part 2Captain verdict

2.1 / Captain verdict

Captain verdict

The family now follows the captain's chosen reading experiences while keeping the unresolved analysis boundaries visible.

  • Decision — approved as-is. Rows remain its one public presentation.
  • DecisionAnalysis — approved visual form, interaction and scoring still open. Four variations below isolate answer behavior, seven-criterion scale, and explicit weighted arithmetic.
  • QuickDecision — one brief call, not a set. It keeps recommendation framing, radio options, proposal, confirmation, and Change; it removes comparison entirely.
  • DecisionMatrix, DecisionBrief, and useComplexMode — still rejected as public API. The first two name presentations, while the boolean would change an entire grammar.

2.1.1 / Why QuickDecision now wins

The taxonomy census still matters: six sets contain 25 questions, and twelve of those questions are too heavy to remain quick. The captain's choice accepts more slides or sub-slides for the genuinely small calls in exchange for a complete interaction around each one. That cost is explicit rather than denied.

The guardrail is now stronger: a QuickDecision must fit one framing sentence and short option labels. Anything needing criteria, score reasons, reversibility, or detailed option prose moves to Decision or DecisionAnalysis.

2.1.2 / Why analysis interaction is not useComplexMode

The first three DecisionAnalysis variations author the same kind of criteria, scores, reasons, reversibility, and options. An interaction="audit" | "choose" prop changes only whether the reader can select and confirm an outcome. Weighted scoring adds explicit impact and 1–5 score fields as a separate evidence capability. Both seams are narrower and more honest than useComplexMode, which would switch the entire child grammar.

Part 3Decision

3.1 / Decision

Decision

Ask the reviewer to choose one answer, explain each option in place, and end with one confirmation.

3.1.1 / Public contract

  • Decision — question: string is required.
  • Option — title: string is required and unique; recommended?: boolean marks at most one; summary?: string adds one-sentence framing.
  • Consideration — optional and option-local; label: string and verdict: string are required, with an optional one-sentence body.

Decision has no layout, status, chosen, Criterion, Score, or useComplexMode prop. The taxonomy report proposed deleting structured considerations entirely; this plan instead keeps a deliberately weaker, option-local form because the captain-approved row treatment depends on concise labels and values. There is no global criterion declaration, completeness rule, tone, or cross-option alignment. Once comparable scoring is the value, the content belongs in DecisionAnalysis.

3.1.2 / Live target appearance

This live example uses today's Decision renderer with layout="rows" as a visual proxy. The recommended contract removes that layout prop because rows become the component's one presentation.

Part 4DecisionAnalysis

4.1 / DecisionAnalysis variations

DecisionAnalysis variations

Four variations isolate the remaining questions: audit versus choose, three versus seven criteria, and qualitative values versus explicit weighted arithmetic.

4.1.1 / Public contract

  • DecisionAnalysis — question: string, state: "proposed" | "decided" | "deferred", and interaction: "audit" | "choose" are required; scoring: "qualitative" | "weighted" defaults to qualitative.
  • Criterion — title: string is required and unique; the body is a required one-sentence meaning; weighted scoring additionally requires impact: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5.
  • Option — title: string is required and unique; recommended?: boolean, chosen?: boolean, and summary?: string carry recommendation, outcome, and synthesis.
  • Score — criterion: string, verdict: string, and tone: "good" | "mixed" | "bad" | "neutral" are required; the body is a one-sentence reason; weighted scoring additionally requires score: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5.
  • Reversibility — rating: "easy" | "somewhat-hard" | "hard" and a concise body describe the cost of changing course.
  • Details — optional body for deeper analysis.

Every option supplies one score for every criterion. There is no layout or useComplexMode prop, and there is no criteria-free fallback. interaction="audit" never renders answer controls. interaction="choose" is valid only for a proposed analysis; confirmation records a reading-session answer without rewriting the authored plan. Weighted scoring makes the formula public: Σ(impact × option score) ÷ Σ(impact × 5), with every input constrained to the same 1–5 scale.

4.1.2 / Variation A — audit only

Recommended. This uses today's settled Decision matrix to render the approved keyed rail, spacing, and definitions. The target replaces disabled radio circles with outcome markers and remains a record, not a form.

The dashed underline remains the definition affordance. A criterion reveals what it means; a score reveals why that value holds. Each reveal stays one sentence maximum on hover, keyboard focus, or tap.

4.1.3 / Variation B — interactive weighted choice

This variation intentionally reopens the round-2 boundary. The keyed matrix is enabled: select an option, confirm it, then use Change to reopen the same scored comparison.

4.1.4 / Variation C — seven-criterion scale test

The same enabled chooser now carries seven criteria, enough to expose scan length, vertical reach, and the cost of keeping every value explained.

4.1.5 / Variation D — full weighted scoring

This enabled chooser adds explicit 1–5 option scores and live 1–5 IMPACT weights; every composite prints its exact arithmetic rather than asking the reviewer to trust a hidden rank.

The four variations preserve the same option identity and one-sentence definition contract while isolating one design question at a time:

  • Audit only: strongest round-2 and taxonomy fit; plan acceptance or feedback settles disagreement outside the matrix.
  • Three-criterion interactive: the approved 4.1.3 baseline and fastest direct comparison.
  • Seven-criterion interactive: exposes how the keyed rail and matrix feel when a real analysis becomes long.
  • Full weighted scoring: lets the reviewer change criterion impact and inspect the exact composite formula before selecting, confirming, or changing the answer.
Part 5QuickDecision

5.1 / QuickDecision

QuickDecision

Give one small call the approved brief interaction, with no comparison disclosure hiding underneath it.

5.1.1 / Public contract

  • QuickDecision — question: string is required; context?: string is one optional framing sentence.
  • Option — title: string is required and unique within the question; recommended?: boolean marks at most one; summary?: string is one optional sentence.

QuickDecision has no set wrapper, criteria, scores, lifecycle state, reversibility, rich option bodies, or comparison expander. It owns native radio selection, Propose another approach, Confirm, the recorded answer, and Change.

5.1.2 / Three live calls in sequence

These use today's Decision brief as live proxies. Each non-discriminating compatibility criterion is invisible and exists only because today's compiler still requires one; the target QuickDecision contract has no criteria and never renders Compare all three.

Three complete briefs make the ergonomic cost visible: each quick call repeats recommendation framing, proposal, confirmation, and Change instead of hiding several answers behind one batch completion.

This is not DecisionBrief as a public root. The component is named QuickDecision because its contract enforces the job: one low-cost call, one sentence of framing, and no deeper comparison path.

Part 6Boundaries

6.1 / Boundaries and migration

Boundaries and migration

Decision and QuickDecision are settled; implementation waits only on the analysis interaction and scoring choices.

6.1.1 / Reconciliation with round 2

  • Preserved in all four variations: Decision remains the ordinary answer component; DecisionAnalysis always requires complete scored evidence.
  • Preserved: shared implementation primitives are allowed, but they do not define the product taxonomy.
  • Variation A: preserves the ruling exactly—Decision asks and DecisionAnalysis records.
  • Variations B and C: deliberately relax the ruling—DecisionAnalysis may ask only after the author has supplied the full analysis grammar; C stress-tests that same behavior at seven criteria.
  • Variation D: keeps the enabled interaction and adds a second explicit choice: whether DecisionAnalysis should own user-adjustable criterion impact and normalized composite scoring.
  • Preserved visually: option identity stays in the full-title keyed rail; criteria and values retain one-sentence dashed-underline definitions.

The interactive variations no longer require one universal Decision: the two components keep different authoring grammars, while interaction="choose" changes only the analysis component's answer behavior. The tradeoff is conceptual, not structural—DecisionAnalysis would own both “audit” and “settle this scored call.” Weighted scoring is separable from that interaction decision because it changes the evidence model, not whether the reviewer can answer.

QuickDecision is a separate, intentionally narrow answer component. It accepts the taxonomy cost of more slides and a relative-weight name in exchange for the captain-approved brief interaction on every small call.

6.1.2 / Internal primitives

  • A selection primitive supports the answer controls used by Decision, QuickDecision, and interactive DecisionAnalysis.
  • A keyed identity rail and comparison matrix support all DecisionAnalysis variations.
  • An optional weighted-score primitive owns native impact controls, transparent arithmetic, and live normalized composites without changing the selection primitive.
  • The brief choice list, proposal, confirmation, recorded answer, and Change behavior are shared by QuickDecision without carrying over its comparison disclosure.
  • Definition popovers remain shared presentation infrastructure for criterion meanings and score reasons.

These are internal seams. They should be extracted only where there are real consumers, and none should force public components to share a grammar.

6.1.3 / Pre-release migration

  1. Make current Decision rows its only public presentation and remove layout, lifecycle, and matrix-only authoring from its contract.
  2. Rename and sharpen ComplexDecision to DecisionAnalysis; require criteria, complete score coverage, state, and reversibility, then implement the selected interaction variation.
  3. Replace SimpleDecisionSet with standalone QuickDecision; migrate each genuinely small question to one brief-minus-compare call and promote the heavier twelve observed questions.
  4. Retire public brief and matrix layouts. Carry their approved visual language into QuickDecision and DecisionAnalysis respectively.
  5. Update every example, test, machine model, and authoring guide as one clean pre-release contract change; add no compatibility aliases.

6.1.4 / Acceptance boundary

Decision and QuickDecision need no further UX choice. For DecisionAnalysis, A versus B decides audit-only versus enabled choice; C shows whether B remains usable at seven criteria; D separately asks whether explicit impact weights and computed composites earn a place in the contract. The artifact still uses Decision as the live proxy, with only the example support needed for empty-brief suppression and transparent weighted scoring; it does not implement the renamed public components.
