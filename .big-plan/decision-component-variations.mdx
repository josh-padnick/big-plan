# Final Decision component direction

The approved row and brief treatments now sit beside the selected keyed matrix, with definitions attached directly to every matrix criterion and value.

<QuickSummary>

<Why>

- A decision should expose just enough evidence to choose without making the reader decode cramped headers or unexplained shorthand.

</Why>

<What>

- One Decision component with three reading depths: rows, keyed matrix, and brief.

</What>

<How>

- Author each criterion meaning and each value reason once, then reveal them from dashed-underlined terms on hover, focus, or tap.

</How>

</QuickSummary>

## The component model

_One Decision component, three layouts; ComplexDecision remains a different component._

All three surfaces below exercise the built-in `Decision` component:

- `layout="rows"` is the approved per-option scan.
- `layout="matrix"` is the selected Matrix 3 keyed chooser rail.
- `layout="brief"` is the approved disclosure-first version.

Matrix 1 and Matrix 2 were exploration-only layouts and are removed. They are not separate components and are no longer authorable.

The approved round-2 boundary remains intact: `Decision` asks the reviewer a question and ends in a confirmed answer, while `ComplexDecision` records a weighted analysis for audit. They stay separate components because they own different jobs, and both build matrix-shaped presentations on the internal `ComparisonMatrix` primitive.

## Authoring contract

_Meanings and reasons belong in the model, so every delivery surface can use them._

`Decision` now declares criteria explicitly, matching the structural clarity already proven by `ComplexDecision`:

```mdx
<Criterion title="Build effort">


The relative implementation and maintenance cost.


</Criterion>

<Consideration criterion="Build effort" verdict="Medium" tone="mixed">


The overlay and tray must coordinate layering and keyboard focus.


</Consideration>
```

The Criterion body is required and explains what the criterion means in one sentence maximum. Each Option supplies one Consideration for every Criterion; its body is required and explains why that verdict holds in one sentence maximum.

This is component behavior, not document-local mock CSS. The default matrix renders the shared popover interaction, while the approved rows and brief layouts retain their lighter visual treatment.
